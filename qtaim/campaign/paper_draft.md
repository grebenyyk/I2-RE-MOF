# I₂@MOF real-space analysis — draft paragraphs

## Discussion (real-space bonding and charge transfer)

Topological (QTAIM), charge-transfer-density, and electron-localization-function (ELF) analyses converge on a consistent picture of the I₂ adsorbate as a linker-templated polyiodide rather than discrete I₂. In all three rare-earth frameworks (Y, Tb, Gd) and both pore states (strained and activated), bond critical points (BCPs) appear between neighbouring iodines with ρ_BCP = 0.025–0.075 e bohr⁻³ — well above the van der Waals contact threshold (≲0.005 e bohr⁻³) — confirming a connected, chemically bonded polyiodide rather than a chain of weakly interacting I₂ molecules. The bond character is pore-geometry dependent. In the strained pores every I–I BCP is closed-shell (∇²ρ > 0, ρ_BCP ≈ 0.037–0.049 e bohr⁻³) at near-uniform distances of ~3 Å, the QTAIM signature of a symmetric, delocalized three-centre–four-electron (3c–4e) interaction — the same bonding found in isolated I₃⁻/I₅⁻, whose I–I BCPs likewise carry ∇²ρ > 0. In the activated pores each chain retains one or two short σ-bonded, near-gas-phase I₂-like cores (∇²ρ ≲ 0, ρ_BCP ≈ 0.07 e bohr⁻³, d ≈ 2.7 Å) plus longer closed-shell links (∇²ρ > 0) that complete an asymmetric [I–I···I]⁻ chain. The ELF is consistent with this topology and is not in conflict with the closed-shell Laplacian of the strained chain: continuous localization basins (ELF > 0.5) run along every I–I contact, with compact lone-pair lobes on iodine, the localization graded — strongest (ELF ≈ 0.9) on the short I₂ cores and weaker but unambiguously present (ELF ≈ 0.65–0.75) along the closed-shell links and across the entire strained chain. The coexistence of ELF > 0.5 with ∇²ρ > 0 is the expected signature of a delocalized 3c–4e / donor–acceptor bond: ELF reports a localized electron pair (a Pauli-repulsion deficit) between the atoms, whereas a positive Laplacian reports charge concentrated into the atomic basins rather than at the BCP. The charge-transfer density, δρ = ρ(MOF+I₂) − ρ(MOF) − ρ(chain), integrated over Bader basins (Σδρ = 0 to numerical precision, confirming an exact partition), quantifies the electronic reorganization: iodine gains 0.3–0.9 e per cell, donated almost entirely by the organic linker (C and O, −0.1 to −0.4 e each), while the rare-earth cation is a spectator (|δq_M| < 0.04 e per atom) for Y, Tb, and Gd alike. The polyiodide is therefore a linker-to-iodine charge-transfer complex, not a metal-redox product. Strained-pore geometries transfer systematically more charge than activated ones (e.g., Gd: +0.93 vs +0.32 e/cell), tracking the stronger strained-pore sorption energies; this trend is mirrored in the bond topology, with the more strongly reduced strained polyiodide adopting the fully delocalized 3c–4e form and the less-reduced activated polyiodide retaining localized I₂ cores — pore geometry, not metal identity, tunes the degree of charge transfer across the isostructural rare-earth series.

The I···O bond critical points locate the iodine–framework contacts at the linker **carboxylate (–COO–) oxygens** (predominantly; a single node μ₃-OH contact appears in Gd-activated), identifying the physical anchor points of the polyiodide. These I···O contacts are all closed-shell (∇²ρ > 0, ρ_BCP ≈ 0.02–0.31 e Å⁻³ over d ≈ 2.5–4.0 Å), characteristic of halogen-bond / n_O → σ*_I donor–acceptor interactions. The projected density of states (PDOS) resolves the orbital mechanism of the charge transfer through these contacts. The iodine 5p manifold presents a low-lying **σ\* acceptor peak immediately above the Fermi level** (E − E_F ≈ 0.1–0.3 eV), distinct from the higher 5p antibonding/conduction states at 2–3 eV; its proximity to E_F makes iodine readily reducible and identifies it as the charge-transfer acceptor. In the strained pore this σ\* acceptor lies lower (≈ 0.13 eV above E_F in Y) and is denser than in the activated pore (≈ 0.24 eV), so charge donation from the carboxylate O-2p valence states is more favorable — consistent with the larger Bader charge transfer to iodine in the strained pore (Y: +0.74 vs +0.50 e/cell), even though the activated pore presents the greater number of I–O contacts (Y: 8 vs 3) and each is individually weaker, so that more anchor points do not yield more transfer. Per-oxygen, the O-2p density of states at the valence edge is in fact higher in the strained pore. The PDOS therefore shows that the degree of charge transfer is **gated by the iodine 5p acceptor level** rather than by the number of I–O contacts: the strained pore yields a more reduced polyiodide because its geometry lowers the I-5p σ\* acceptor and couples it more strongly to the linker-oxygen valence states.

## Computational details (real-space analysis)

Real-space descriptors were obtained from tight single-point SCF calculations on the converged I₂-loaded geometries and on the corresponding empty framework and bare iodine-chain references, all at the same atomic positions (framework frozen at the loaded geometry) so that the density difference isolates electronic reorganization from structural relaxation. We used PBE + D3(BJ) with PAW pseudopotentials (C.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl, H/O.pbe-kjpaw, Y.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl, Tb/Gd.pbe-spdn-kjpaw_psl, I.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl), ecutwfc = 60 Ry, ecutrho = 800 Ry, Γ-point sampling, Marzari–Vanderbilt cold smearing (degauss = 0.01 Ry), and conv_thr = 10⁻⁸ (the Gd loaded SCF, a small-gap charge-transfer state, required mixing_beta = 0.1 and conv_thr = 10⁻⁶ to converge; the resulting valence densities are equivalently converged for topological analysis). Valence charge-density (plot_num = 0), PAW-reconstructed valence (plot_num = 17), and ELF (plot_num = 8) cubes were generated with pp.x on the 160 × 180 × 300 FFT grid. Bond critical points and Bader basins were located with critic2 (v1.2.1005) on the plot_num = 17 field; the charge-transfer density was integrated over these basins. A small number of intra-atomic (3,−1) critical points in iodine's 5p-shell region — identifiable by gradient paths terminating at the same nucleus or at non-nuclear maxima, with ρ ≈ 0.10 e bohr⁻³ outside the bonding range — were excluded as artefacts of the PAW reconstruction, and the reported ρ_BCP and ∇²ρ values are reproduced on a PAW-augmentation-free valence field (plot_num = 0) and by an independent bond-path ρ(r) profile. Local EML bond energies (D_e = ½|V(r_BCP)|) are not reported: the empirical ½ factor was calibrated on light-atom and hydrogen-bonded systems and is not reliable for heavy iodine, compounded by the Abramov kinetic-energy-density approximation that would otherwise be required (the QE plot_num = 9 kinetic-energy cube is unreliable for PAW potentials). ELF is reported as the "soft" (PAW-smoothed) quantity, which is well-defined in the bonding and lone-pair regions shown. All analyses were performed for Y, Tb, and Gd in both the strained (guest-imposed) and activated (relaxed-empty) pore geometries. Projected densities of states were computed with projwfc.x from the wavefunctions of the loaded SCFs (Gaussian broadening, degauss = 0.01 Ry), summing the I-5p contribution over all iodine atoms and the O-2p contribution over the carboxylate oxygens that bear I–O bond critical points; energies are referenced to the Fermi level of each system.
