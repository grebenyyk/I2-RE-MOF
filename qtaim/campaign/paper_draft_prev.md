# I₂@MOF real-space analysis — draft paragraphs

## Discussion (real-space bonding and charge transfer)

Topological (QTAIM), charge-transfer-density, and electron-localization-function (ELF) analyses converge on a consistent picture of the I₂ adsorbate as a linker-templated polyiodide rather than discrete I₂. In all three rare-earth frameworks (Y, Tb, Gd) and both pore states (strained and activated), bond critical points (BCPs) appear between neighbouring iodines in two distinct populations: short, shared-shell contacts (ρ_BCP ≈ 0.09–0.11 e bohr⁻³, ∇²ρ < 0) corresponding to I₂-like pairs, and longer closed-shell contacts (ρ_BCP ≈ 0.04–0.06 e bohr⁻³, ∇²ρ > 0) that knit the pairs into an I₃⁻/I₅⁻-type chain. The fourth, "branching" iodine attaches to the chain through one of these closed-shell (halogen-bond-like) contacts — it is not covalently bound to the backbone; covalent character is confined to the I₂ pairs. The ELF confirms this topology: continuous localization basins (ELF > 0.5) run along every I–I contact, with compact lone-pair lobes on iodine, and the localization is graded — strongest in the I₂ pairs, weaker but unambiguously present in the closed-shell links. The charge-transfer density, δρ = ρ(MOF+I₂) − ρ(MOF) − ρ(chain), integrated over Bader basins (Σδρ = 0 to numerical precision, confirming an exact partition), quantifies the electronic reorganization: iodine gains 0.3–0.9 e per cell, and this charge is donated almost entirely by the organic linker (C and O, −0.1 to −0.4 e each), while the rare-earth cation is a spectator (|δq_M| < 0.04 e per atom) for Y, Tb, and Gd alike. The polyiodide is therefore a linker-to-iodine charge-transfer complex, not a metal-redox product. Strained-pore geometries transfer systematically more charge than activated ones (e.g., Gd: +0.93 vs +0.32 e/cell), tracking the stronger strained-pore sorption energies and confirming that pore geometry — not the metal identity — tunes the degree of charge transfer across the isostructural rare-earth series.

The I···O bond critical points locate the iodine–framework contacts at the linker **carboxylate (–COO–) oxygens** (15 of 16 I–O BCPs; a single node μ₃-OH contact appears in Gd-activated), identifying the physical anchor points of the polyiodide. The projected density of states (PDOS) resolves the orbital mechanism of the charge transfer through these contacts. The iodine 5p manifold presents a low-lying **σ\* acceptor peak immediately above the Fermi level** (E − E_F ≈ 0.1–0.3 eV), distinct from the higher 5p antibonding/conduction states at 2–3 eV; its proximity to E_F makes iodine readily reducible and identifies it as the charge-transfer acceptor. In the strained pore this σ\* acceptor lies lower (≈ 0.13 eV above E_F in Y) and is denser than in the activated pore (≈ 0.24 eV), so charge donation from the carboxylate O-2p valence states is more favorable — consistent with the larger Bader charge transfer to iodine in the strained pore (Y: +0.74 vs +0.50 e/cell), even though the activated pore presents *more* I–O contacts (more carboxylate oxygens reach the iodine). Per-oxygen, the O-2p density of states at the valence edge is in fact higher in the strained pore. The PDOS therefore shows that the degree of charge transfer is **gated by the iodine 5p acceptor level** rather than by the number of I–O contacts: the strained pore yields a more reduced polyiodide because its geometry lowers the I-5p σ\* acceptor and couples it more strongly to the linker-oxygen valence states.

## Computational details (real-space analysis)

Real-space descriptors were obtained from tight single-point SCF calculations on the converged I₂-loaded geometries and on the corresponding empty framework and bare iodine-chain references, all at the same atomic positions (framework frozen at the loaded geometry) so that the density difference isolates electronic reorganization from structural relaxation. We used PBE + D3(BJ) with PAW pseudopotentials (C.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl, H/O.pbe-kjpaw, Y.pbe-spn-kjpaw_psl, Tb/Gd.pbe-spdn-kjpaw_psl, I.pbe-n-kjpaw_psl), ecutwfc = 60 Ry, ecutrho = 800 Ry, Γ-point sampling, Marzari–Vanderbilt cold smearing (degauss = 0.01 Ry), and conv_thr = 10⁻⁸ (the Gd loaded SCF, a small-gap charge-transfer state, required mixing_beta = 0.1 and conv_thr = 10⁻⁶ to converge; the resulting valence densities are equivalently converged for topological analysis). Valence charge-density (plot_num = 0), PAW-reconstructed valence (plot_num = 17), and ELF (plot_num = 8) cubes were generated with pp.x on the 160 × 180 × 300 FFT grid. Bond critical points and Bader basins were located with critic2 (v1.2.1005) on the plot_num = 17 field; the charge-transfer density was integrated over these basins. Local bond energies at the BCPs were estimated with the Espinosa–Molins–Lecomte relation, D_e = ½|V(r_BCP)|, using the Abramov approximation for the kinetic energy density G = (3/10)(3π²)^(2/3)ρ^(5/3) + (1/6)∇²ρ and the local virial V = ¼∇²ρ − 2G (the QE kinetic-energy-density cube, plot_num = 9, is unreliable for PAW potentials and was not used). ELF is reported as the "soft" (PAW-smoothed) quantity, which is well-defined in the bonding and lone-pair regions shown. All analyses were performed for Y, Tb, and Gd in both the strained (guest-imposed) and activated (relaxed-empty) pore geometries. Projected densities of states were computed with projwfc.x from the wavefunctions of the loaded SCFs (Gaussian broadening, degauss = 0.01 Ry), summing the I-5p contribution over all iodine atoms and the O-2p contribution over the carboxylate oxygens that bear I–O bond critical points; energies are referenced to the Fermi level of each system.
